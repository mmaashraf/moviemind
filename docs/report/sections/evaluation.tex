\section{Evaluation}

\subsection{Raw machine learning results (test set)}

Table~\ref{tab:ml-raw} summarizes models trained with default or project-standard hyperparameters.

\begin{table}[ht]
  \centering
  \caption{Raw ML and baseline metrics on the test split (70/10/20 protocol).}
  \label{tab:ml-raw}
  \begin{tabular}{lrrr}
    \toprule
    Model & RMSE & MAE & Fit time (s) \\
    \midrule
    Baseline (global mean) & 1.1048 & 0.9181 & 0.00 \\
    Linear regression      & 0.9005 & 0.7088 & 0.06 \\
    Random forest (raw)    & 0.8998 & 0.7084 & 20.72 \\
    \textbf{Gradient boosting (raw)} & \textbf{0.8981} & \textbf{0.7062} & 170.06 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Gradient boosting achieves the lowest test RMSE and MAE among all families.
Linear regression and random forest are competitive, confirming that engineered aggregates capture much of
the signal in MovieLens 1M.

\subsection{Tuned machine learning}

Validation-selected hyperparameters for random forest and gradient boosting yield test RMSE 0.8998 and
0.8993 respectively---slightly \emph{worse} than the raw gradient boosting run (Table~B in project evidence).
Fast-pass subsampled tuning did not improve the champion; raw GB remains the reporting winner.

\subsection{Champion selection}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Overall offline champion:} raw Gradient Boosting (test RMSE 0.8981).
  \item \textbf{Best DL path:} Optuna-tuned NCF on validation objective.
  \item \textbf{Trade-off:} GB training takes ${\sim}$170\,s vs.\ $<$1\,s for linear regression; NCF tuning
        adds ${\sim}$16 minutes of trial search in our recorded run.
\end{itemize}
